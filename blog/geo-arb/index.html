Geographic arbitrage for European SWEs

The Levels.fyi Europe heatmap shows how software engineer pay varies across Europe, but it doesn’t tell you how much you’d have left after taxes and living costs. I used it as a starting point to compare what you could save each month in six cities.

I took five pay percentiles from Levels.fyi in November 2025 and modeled taxes and employee contributions in Python. I worked from published government code where I could and used Claude to help translate the written rules where I couldn’t. To estimate monthly savings, I divided annual take-home pay by 12 and subtracted a budget based on Numbeo for one person renting a one-bedroom apartment in the city centre.

  • I paired country-level pay with city-level costs, so the same pay percentile can reflect a different mix of jobs and experience in each country.
  • I spread stock and bonuses across the year as if they were cash, even though they don’t necessarily come in as regular monthly income.
  • For Vaduz, I used Swiss pay as a stand-in because I didn’t have enough data for Liechtenstein.

The black line marks gross pay, and the colors show how much goes to taxes and contributions, how much goes to living costs, and what’s left. If take-home pay doesn’t cover all the living costs, the bar shows only what you can afford, while the table shows the full costs and how much you’d be short.
