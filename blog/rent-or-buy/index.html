Rent or Buy

I compared buying a €500,000 flat in Vienna with renting and investing from 1994 through 2024. Both strategies use the same salary and starting savings.

I estimate take-home pay using modeled Austrian income tax and employee contributions, then set aside €480 a month for costs outside housing. Both households use what’s left for housing and investing.

The renter pays €950 a month for rent and utilities and invests the rest, along with the initial savings, in the iShares MSCI ACWI UCITS ETF (Acc).

The buyer pays purchase costs first and uses the rest as the down payment, which sets the loan size and interest rate. The payoff time depends on how much the buyer puts down and how much they can pay each month after running costs. The dashed line marks the month the loan is paid off, after which the buyer invests the surplus in the same ETF.

Each point shows net worth after that month’s payments and investments. For the buyer, I use the Vienna apartment price index to estimate the flat’s value, subtract the remaining mortgage, and add cash and investments. ETF values use Curvo’s backtest and account for modeled taxes and trading costs, while the flat’s value excludes selling costs. Within each scenario the monthly budget stays fixed, and I don’t adjust for inflation.
